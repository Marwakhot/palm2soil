Why we ask

What your records are for

The gap we are closing

The satellite measures how a field reflects light. It cannot measure salt content directly. To turn a reflectance measurement into a usable number, the system needs matched pairs: what the satellite saw, and what was actually on the ground.

Right now we have none.The last soil survey at scale was in 2009 and is only available as regional percentages. Until paired observations exist, the system can rank fields against each other but cannot state a soil value.

What each record contributes

1
Field photos — confirm whether a flagged field looks stressed on the ground, or whether the signal came from crop type or growth stage.
2
Irrigation log — separates water shortage from soil condition. Both lower the same index.
3
Advisor visit — attaches an expert judgement, and a soil sample where one is taken, to a specific field and date.

Records are tied to a field and a date so they can be matched to the scan. They are not used to assess or rank individual farms.

Support

Before acting on a flag

Talk to your advisor first

A flag means a field is worth checking. It does not say what is wrong or what to apply.

If a soil amendment is proposed

1
Confirm the field and plan with an advisor.
2
Check the product and the application rate. Some biochar carries high conductivity of its own.
3
Record what was applied and when, so recovery can be checked on a later scan.

The app supports farm visits and follow-up. It should never be used to self-treat from a satellite alert alone.
